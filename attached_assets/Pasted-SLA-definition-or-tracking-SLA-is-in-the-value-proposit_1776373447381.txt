SLA definition or tracking - SLA is in the value proposition. It must be in the product and I am happy with the initial suggested approach.

The scheduling board - There are three industry-proven patterns to consider. A pure Gantt (jobs as horizontal time bars) is great for showing duration and overlap but gets unwieldy with many resources. A pure calendar grid (resources as rows, days as columns) is how most field service tools like ServiceMax and Skedulo work — it's the most natural for this use case. A split-panel approach combines an unassigned jobs queue on the left with the calendar grid on the right, which is what I want here because "unassigned jobs" is your most critical operational alert.
The split-panel with a resource-row calendar is I think the right choice for Jiganto. Attached is an interactive mockup of how it could look and behave.
You can drag any job card from the left queue onto a resource row, click any allocated block to see its detail panel, and use the filter pills and week navigation. Here's the reasoning behind every design decision:
The unassigned jobs queue is the most important element on the screen. Every minute an unassigned job sits there is operational risk. Keeping it permanently visible on the left — rather than buried in a filter — means planners are always aware of the backlog. The badge count and colour-coded priority stripes (red/blue/grey) let a planner triage the queue at a glance before touching anything.
Drag and drop is the primary allocation gesture. This mirrors industry tools like Skedulo and ServiceTitan. The planner drags a job from the queue onto a resource's day cell. The cell highlights on hover to confirm the drop zone is valid. This is intuitive and fast — much faster than a modal form.
Conflict detection is proactive, not reactive. Sarah Chen's Wednesday shows both the red cell border and the warning banner at the top. The planner doesn't need to hunt for problems — the board surfaces them. The "Resolve conflict" button triggers a natural language query to the matching engine asking for alternatives.
Utilisation bars on each resource row are a critical operational metric that most scheduling tools bury in a separate report. Seeing 40% next to Raj Patel immediately tells a planner that capacity is available there. The red bar on Sarah Chen reinforces the conflict visually.
Unavailability uses a hatch pattern, not a colour. Lisa O'Brien's entire row is hatched to show annual leave. This means it reads clearly in both light and dark mode, and doesn't get confused with status colours.
