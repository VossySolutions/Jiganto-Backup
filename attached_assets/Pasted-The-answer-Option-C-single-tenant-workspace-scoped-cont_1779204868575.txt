The answer: Option C — single tenant, workspace-scoped context switching
Neither of the obvious approaches is right on its own.
Option A (separate tenant per customer) means your SI consultant needs to log into three different URLs to manage three customers. There's no master PMO dashboard, no unified reporting, and you multiply your infrastructure costs by the number of customers. This is how old-school SaaS worked in the 2010s — it's not how modern platforms are built.
Option B (one tenant, filter by tag) is what most people reach for first and it has a fatal flaw: data isolation is enforced only by a WHERE clause. If a bug in your filter logic fires incorrectly, Customer A's users could momentarily see Customer B's projects. For enterprise clients — especially in regulated sectors like NHS, finance, or public sector — that's a showstopper. It also makes it technically very difficult to give each customer a clean, branded experience.
The recommended approach is a single Jiganto organisation with workspace-level data isolation and a context switcher in the navigation bar. Here's exactly how it works:
Workspaces as the isolation unit. Each customer gets their own Workspace inside your Jiganto org. Access control is enforced at the workspace level by the permission system — not by filtering. A Globex user's session literally cannot read Acme Corp data because they have no permission token for that workspace, not because a filter is excluding it. This is architecturally secure.
The context switcher replaces the current "Demo Org" pill. You already have this pill in the top-left of the Jiganto nav. It currently just shows the org name. Extend it into a full workspace switcher dropdown — the interactive demo above shows exactly what this looks like. When Peter Voss is about to sit down with Acme Corp, he clicks the pill, selects "Acme Corp workspace," and the entire UI — projects, tasks, documents, help desk tickets, CRM contacts — immediately scopes to only Acme Corp data. The URL can optionally change to app.jiganto.com/ws/acme-corp for shareable deep links.
Two distinct user types with different default experiences. SI consultants have cross-workspace access and always land on the master org view. Client users — Acme Corp's project manager, for example — log in with their own credentials and land directly in their workspace with no switcher visible at all. They have no awareness that other customers exist. When you're sitting with a client, you switch into their workspace and your screen looks identical to what they see on their own login.
The PMO master dashboard aggregates across all workspaces. This is only visible to SI admin roles. It's the Command Centre layout from the Projects redesign — all 14 projects, all customers, team utilisation, at-risk alerts — pulling data across every workspace simultaneously.
